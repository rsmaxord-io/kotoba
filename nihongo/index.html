Before you begin

Honest answers.

No “fluent in 30 days”, and no pretending Kansai-ben is the only Japanese. Just a clear idea of what this site can and cannot do for you.

Do I need to learn kanji before I can speak?

No. Speaking and reading are separate skills, and you can hold a simple conversation entirely in kana. This site teaches kana and spoken patterns first, then adds 56 basic kanji once the sounds are familiar — the last 24 inside full example sentences. Learn them with words, not as a separate mountain.

Which kind of Japanese do you teach?

Standard Japanese is the main line; Osaka-leaning Kansai-ben is the option underneath it. Every phrase leads with the standard form and adds a Kansai version below, and every dialogue is standard first with the Kansai version in a fold-out. That way you can use です／ます in a formal situation and switch to dialect when the room is relaxed. Kansai-ben is a family, not one accent — Kyoto and Kobe have their own flavours, and locals mix dialect with standard Japanese all the time.

What about Kyoto, Kobe, Nagoya or Hiroshima?

Kansai-ben is a family, not one accent: Kyoto is softer, Kobe has its own lilt, and Nagoya and Hiroshima sit outside Kansai altogether. The dialect map covers all nine regions, with local phrases and standard equivalents, so you can hear the difference rather than just read about it.

How long until I can have a conversation?

For a short transactional conversation — ordering, asking directions, introducing yourself — most learners need a few weeks of regular practice. For a comfortable everyday conversation, think in months, not days. Ten focused minutes a day is a realistic start.

Is the audio a real human voice?

The play buttons use bundled Japanese neural voices, not the robotic voice your browser happens to have installed: a clear standard voice for the main lines, and a slower, lower Osaka-style male voice for Kansai lines and the dialect map. They are studio-quality approximations, not live human recordings, and a neural voice cannot reproduce a true Osaka accent. Use them to check the words and rhythm, then copy the melody from Kansai speakers, radio or TV. Nothing is uploaded and no account is needed.

Is this a replacement for a teacher or textbook?

It is a full structured course, but not a replacement for a person. The 100 levels take you from the five vowels to N1 grammar, with writing and speaking tasks from level 51, and every line has audio. What no site can give you is someone reacting to your mistakes in real time. Use the levels to arrive at class with better questions; a teacher or a patient native speaker is still the fastest way to fix pronunciation.
