Two alphabets.
Forty-six sounds.

Standard Japanese and Kansai-ben use exactly the same kana — the characters do not change, only the words and the melody. Hiragana handles grammar and native words; katakana handles loanwords and emphasis. Both are phonetic: one character, one sound, every time. Together that is ninety-two characters for forty-six core sounds. Tap any character to hear it. When you are ready, switch to the quiz.

Start with five

You do not need all 46 today. Learn one row — あ い う え お — say each one aloud, then take the quiz. That first win matters more than a perfect plan.

How to learn kana

  • Write each character by hand, saying it out loud.
  • Test recall, not recognition. Cover the romaji.
  • Learn a row a day, revise the row before it.
  • Read real words as soon as you can: すし, カフェ.

Hiragana or katakana?

Hiragana is the rounded script for grammar and everyday Japanese words. Katakana is the angular script for foreign words — コーヒー, パン, your own name — plus sound effects and emphasis.

Learn hiragana first. Katakana will arrive much faster once the sounds are familiar.

Standard and Kansai, same kana

Dialect words are written in the ordinary kana you are learning here: まいど, おおきに, あかん, なんぼ. No extra alphabet, no special font.

What changes is the sound of the sentence — the pitch melody and the endings. Learn the characters here, then borrow the tune from Kansai speakers.

Three small warnings

  • を is written “wo” but pronounced like “o”.
  • ん is a full beat of its own — almost like a soft “n” hum.
  • Long vowels change meaning: おばさん (aunt) vs おばあさん (grandmother).
