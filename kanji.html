Step four · 漢字

Fifty-six characters.
A wider city.

Kana tells you how a word sounds. Kanji tells you what it means. The first 32 characters cover numbers, days, people, directions, everyday actions and money. Then 24 more appear inside full sentences — school, home, travel, weather, animals and the verbs you use every day. Standard Japanese and Kansai-ben use the same characters; only the grammar around them changes.

漢字

How to read a card

On readings come from Chinese and usually appear in compounds: 日本 is にほん, 学校 is がっこう. Kun readings are native Japanese and often stand alone: 人 is ひと, 水 is みず.

Do not try to memorise every reading at once. Learn one word per character, say it out loud with the audio, and let the other readings arrive later. A character you can recognise in a shop sign is already doing useful work.

Readings here are standard Japanese. Kansai uses the same characters and the same readings; what changes is the grammar around them, shown in the comparison below.

The Kansai twist

Kanji readings do not change in Kansai. 行く is still read いく, and 食べる is still たべる.

What changes is the grammar around them: 行くで, 行かへん, 食べへん, 見てな. Learn the character first, then dress it in the local ending.

Numbers in context

Same number, same kanji, two endings.

The counting words above are standard Japanese. In a full sentence, standard and Kansai share the characters and readings — only the ending changes. Here are four everyday pairs, with the standard line first and its own audio.

Standard first · Kansai option
Standard · 標準語Kansai · 関西弁English
いくらですか。 なんぼですか。 How much is it?
ひとつください。 ひとつ、たのんます。 One, please.
ひゃくえんです。 ひゃくえんやで。 It's 100 yen.
ありがとうございます。 おおきに。 Thank you.

The numbers do not become “Kansai numbers”. 百 is still ひゃく and 一つ is still ひとつ; the dialect lives in です／や、ください／たのんます and the closing particle.

How much kanji is enough? These 56 cover a surprising amount of daily signage: prices, dates, entrances and exits, opening hours, stations, weather and the verbs on a menu. Learn them in words and sentences, not in isolation, and add new characters as you meet them. Reading a city is a long project, but it starts with the first sign you recognise.
