Appendix

Sources, definitions, and what this doesn't prove.

Every figure on this site traces to a named survey or a report of one. Where the numbers disagree, that is usually because they measure different things — and the difference is often the point.

Primary and secondary sources

Listed in order of first use. Survey figures are as reported; where a primary report is Japanese-only, the English reporting is cited alongside it.

  1. Anime Industry Report 2025 (FY2024 data), Association of Japanese Animations (AJA). Total market ¥3.8407 trillion; +14.8% year on year; overseas ¥2.17 trillion (56%, +26%); domestic ¥1.67 trillion (44%, +2.8%). Summary PDFs at aja.gr.jp.
  2. The Hollywood Reporter, “Japan’s Anime Industry Was Worth $25 Billion in 2024,” October 2025. Reporting of the AJA preview, including Masahiko Hasegawa’s comments on overseas revenue and bundled contracts.
  3. Animation Magazine, “AJA Reports Record Year for Japanese Anime; Issues GenAI Statement,” October 2025. Context on the government’s Cool Japan content-industry targets for 2033.
  4. Teikoku Databank, survey of anime production companies (177 firms), reported via Automaton West, 2025. Domestic anime production market ≈ US$2.45 billion (≈ ¥340 billion) in 2024, reported as +4% year on year; 60% of prime/gross contractors with lower profits or losses; manpower shortage as cited cause.
  5. Anime News Network’s AnimeNomics, “Labor deficit muddles anime studio financials,” 2025. Share of lead studios reporting higher profits down more than 13 points to 40%; subcontractor average revenue ¥438 million (+14%); 45.2% of studios transacting with foreign companies.
  6. Anime News Network, “Teikoku Databank: Anime Production Industry Grew 22% in 2023,” October 2024. Production market ¥339 billion in 2023, with an earlier projection of ≈ ¥340 billion for 2024; 37.5% of specialised studios profitable, 43.1% in the red.
  7. Japan Animation Creators Association (JAniCA), Animator Survey series (2009 white paper; 2023 survey), as reported by Nippon.com, “Labor Challenges in Japan’s Anime Industry.” 2023 average annual income: ¥2.63 million for dōga staff, just under ¥4 million for genga staff, ¥4.56 million industry-wide; 2009 dōga figure ¥1.1 million; monthly hours 273 (2008), 263 (2013), 231 (2017), 198 (2021).
  8. NAFCA (Nippon Anime & Film Culture Association) worker survey, reported by Anime News Network’s AnimeNomics. 323 respondents; average 219 monthly hours, ~34% above the national 163.5; median hourly rate ¥1,111 against Tokyo’s ¥1,113 minimum; 85% of studios located in Tokyo.
  9. Fullfrontal.moe, “Production Committees — Understanding the Anime Business Model.” Structure and history of the committee system; studios as subcontractors rather than committee members.
  10. Travesia, “What is the Production Committee System? Basic Structure and Historical Explanation for Overseas Companies.” Per-cour production cost estimate of ¥100–300 million for 13 episodes; risk distribution and rights allocation.
  11. Sakuga Blog, “What Is An Anime’s Production Committee?” (2017). Committee listing order as an indicator of relative investment; typical member composition.
  12. The Japan Times, “Anime’s boom finally pays off — for some,” September 2026. Uneven distribution of recent wage and condition improvements; government revenue targets.
02

Method and limits

Read this before quoting any single number from the site.

Two markets, two definitions

AJA’s ¥3.84 trillion is a broad market measure: it aggregates revenue across the commercial footprint of anime, including licensing, merchandising, events and overseas distribution. Teikoku Databank’s ≈ ¥340 billion measures production revenue invoiced by studios. They are not rival estimates and should not be added, subtracted, or treated as a margin calculation.

Survey samples differ

JAniCA surveys its membership over time, producing a broad longitudinal picture. NAFCA surveyed 323 workers directly, a smaller and self-selected group. The two produce different hours figures — 198 versus 219 — for overlapping periods. Both are reported here, with the discrepancy noted rather than smoothed away.

Rounding

Figures are rounded for readability: ¥3.8407 trillion is shown as ¥3.84T; ¥339–340 billion as ¥340B. Percentages are rounded to the nearest point. Where a figure is an estimate by the reporting organisation, it is labelled as such.

What this site does not claim

It does not claim that every studio is unprofitable, that every animator is underpaid, or that the committee system has no benefits. It claims that the industry’s structure systematically routes the growth in value away from the production layer, and it presents the survey data that supports that claim.

Corrections

If a figure here is wrong or out of date, it should be corrected rather than defended. The primary reports are linked in the list above; check them first.

Access note

Several AJA and Teikoku reports are published in Japanese only, or behind a paid report. English-language summaries and trade reporting are cited where the primary document could not be read directly. This is a limitation, and it is disclosed rather than hidden.
