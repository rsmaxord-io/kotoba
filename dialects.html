01

Hokkaido 北海道

Sapporo 北海道 · Hokkaido

なまら

Japan’s north island, settled widely in the Meiji era. Its speech borrows from Tohoku and adds a frontier directness of its own. Sapporo is the urban anchor; the countryside keeps thicker local colour.

Local→Standard Japanese

なまらnamara
→
とてもvery

Put it before an adjective: なまらうまい = really tasty. It sounds broad and cheerful.

したっけshittake
→
じゃあねsee you

A casual goodbye, and also a connector meaning “so then”. It is not the standard だったっけ question.

Hokkaido also shares いずい (“doesn’t feel right”) with Tohoku — dialects travel with the people who settle.

Local note. Sapporo’s speech is the easiest entry point; the fishing towns and farming valleys keep stronger local habits.

Say a whole sentence

Local

なまら さむい けど、たべものは うまいべ。

Standard

とても さむい けれど、たべものは おいしいでしょう。

It is really cold, but the food is good, right?

Local

したっけ、また あした！

Standard

じゃあ、また あした！

See you tomorrow!

02

Tohoku 東北

Sendai 宮城県 · Tohoku

いずい

Six prefectures of mountains, valleys and a famous reputation for soft, fast speech. Sendai is the urban anchor; inland and coastal varieties differ sharply.

Local→Standard Japanese

いずいizui
→
しっくりこないdoesn’t feel right

A shoe that rubs, a room that feels wrong, a person you do not click with.

めんこいmenkoi
→
かわいいcute

Affectionate, often for children, pets and small things.

んだnda
→
そうだthat’s right

The all-purpose Tohoku agreement. A rising んだ？ can also mean “really?”.

The 〜べ ending is another badge of Tohoku speech. You will hear both んだ and 〜べ in Sendai, especially among older speakers.

Local note. Tohoku is often described as soft and fast, but the region is huge: Aomori, Iwate, Akita, Yamagata, Miyagi and Fukushima all have their own flavour.

Say a whole sentence

Local

んだ んだ、めんこい べこ だなあ。

Standard

そうそう、かわいい うし ですね。

Right, what a cute cow.

Local

いずい かも しれね けど、やってみっぺ。

Standard

いごこちが わるい かも しれない けど、やってみよう。

It might feel awkward, but let us try.

03

Kanto 関東

Tokyo 東京都 · Kanto

じゃん

Tokyo, Yokohama and the plain around them. Standard Japanese grew up here, so local speech hides in casual endings rather than whole words.

Local→Standard Japanese

〜じゃんjan
→
〜だよねright? / isn’t it?

A casual tag that softens a statement. Originally Kanto, now heard nationwide.

あたぼうよatabō yo
→
もちろんですof course

Old Edo bravado. Today it lives mostly in period dramas, jokes and a certain Tokyo swagger.

Because Tokyo speech became the standard, Kanto dialect is easy to miss. Listen for sentence endings, not just vocabulary.

Local note. Tokyo’s traditional Edokko speech is quite different from the neutral newscaster Japanese most learners meet.

Say a whole sentence

Local

これ、うまいじゃん。あたぼうよ。

Standard

これ、おいしいね。もちろん。

This is tasty, right? Of course.

Local

そうじゃん、いこうよ。

Standard

そうだね、いこうよ。

Right, let us go.

04

Chubu 中部

Nagoya 愛知県 · Chubu

やっとかめ

A wide middle band from the Japan Sea to the Pacific, with Nagoya at its centre. The mountains keep local words alive; Nagoya’s やっとかめ is the region’s most famous export.

Local→Standard Japanese

やっとかめyattokame
→
おひさしぶりですlong time no see

Nagoya’s warm “it’s been ages”. Older speakers add だなも.

でらdera
→
とてもvery

Nagoya’s intensifier: でらうまい = really tasty.

Nagoya is in Chubu, not Kansai. Its rhythm, endings and vocabulary are their own thing, even if it sits between the two.

Local note. The Japan Sea side, the Japan Alps and the Pacific coast do not sound the same. Chubu is a band, not a single voice.

Say a whole sentence

Local

やっとかめ だなも。でら うまい みそかつ たべていきな。

Standard

おひさしぶり ですね。とても おいしい みそかつを たべて いって ください。

Long time no see. Please stay and eat some really good miso katsu.

Local

でら あつい で、はやく かえろう。

Standard

とても あつい から、はやく かえろう。

It is really hot, so let us go home early.

05

Kansai 関西

Osaka 大阪府 · Kansai

まいど

Osaka, Kyoto, Kobe and the surrounding prefectures. The site’s home dialect: fast, musical and full of contractions. Osaka is blunt and warm; Kyoto is softer and more indirect.

Local→Standard Japanese

まいどmaido
→
こんにちはhello

A shopkeeper’s hello, or a friendly “hey” between regulars.

おおきにōkini
→
ありがとうございますthank you

Osaka’s classic thank you. まいどおおきに is warmer still.

おくれやすokureyasu
→
くださいplease (do)

Kyoto’s polite “please do”. Softer and more indirect than ください.

めっちゃmetcha
→
とてもvery

The Kansai intensifier that went nationwide: めっちゃうまい = really tasty.

Kansai-ben is a family: Osaka is blunt and warm, Kyoto is softer, and Kobe has its own lilt. Locals mix all of it with standard Japanese.

Local note. The standard track on this site is your safe fallback; the Kansai lines are the local colour you hear around Osaka.

Say a whole sentence

Local

まいど、おおきに。なに たべる？

Standard

いらっしゃい、ありがとう。なにを たべますか。

Welcome, thanks. What will you eat?

Local

めっちゃ おいしいやん。もっと おいで。

Standard

とても おいしい ですね。もっと きて ください。

It is really delicious. Come again.

Local

あかん、でんしゃ いま でたわ。

Standard

しまった、でんしゃが いま でました。

Oh no, the train just left.

06

Chugoku 中国

Hiroshima 広島県 · Chugoku

ぶち

The western Honshu strip from Tottori to Yamaguchi. Hiroshima’s ぶち and じゃけん are the best-known markers; the Japan Sea side has quieter patterns of its own.

Local→Standard Japanese

ぶちbuchi
→
とてもvery

Hiroshima’s “very”: ぶちうまい = really tasty.

じゃけんjaken
→
だからso / because

Hiroshima’s “so” or “because”. Often starts the sentence.

たいぎいtaigii
→
めんどくさいa hassle

Hiroshima’s “tiring” or “annoying”. Usually said with a sigh.

Hiroshima-ben is the region’s best-known variety. Yamaguchi and the San’in coast have their own endings and melodies.

Local note. Hiroshima’s じゃけん is a reason-link, not a swear. Tone does a lot of the work.

Say a whole sentence

Local

ぶち うまい じゃけん、もう いっぺん たべたい。

Standard

とても おいしい から、もう いちど たべたい。

It is really tasty, so I want to eat it again.

Local

たいぎい けど、がんばるわ。

Standard

めんどう だけど、がんばるよ。

It is a hassle, but I will do my best.

07

Shikoku 四国

Kochi 高知県 · Shikoku

まっこと

Four prefectures and four distinct flavours. Kochi’s Tosa-ben is the boldest; Kagawa, Ehime and Tokushima all lean toward Kansai in different ways.

Local→Standard Japanese

まっことmakkoto
→
ほんとうにreally

Kochi’s “really”: まっことその通り = that’s absolutely right.

こじゃんとkojanto
→
たくさんa lot

Kochi’s “a lot” or “very much”: こじゃんと食べてよ = eat plenty.

Tosa-ben loves 〜き for “because”: 忙しいき = because I’m busy. The island’s four prefectures do not sound alike.

Local note. Kochi is on the Pacific edge, and its speech has a reputation for being direct, warm and a little theatrical.

Say a whole sentence

Local

まっこと こじゃんと おいしい き、もっと たべや。

Standard

ほんとうに たくさん おいしい から、もっと たべなさい。

It is really very tasty, so eat more.

Local

こじゃんと あそんだ き、つかれた。

Standard

たくさん あそんだ から、つかれた。

I played a lot, so I am tired.

08

Kyushu 九州

Fukuoka 福岡県 · Kyushu

ばり

Seven prefectures with strong identities. Fukuoka’s Hakata-ben is the urban benchmark; Kagoshima and Miyazaki can be much harder for Tokyo ears.

Local→Standard Japanese

ばりbari
→
とてもvery

Hakata’s “very”: ばりうまい = really tasty.

なんしよーとnanshiyō to
→
なにしてるのwhat are you doing?

Fukuoka’s friendly “what are you up to?”. The 〜と ending marks the question.

ばいbai
→
だよyou know / right

A sentence-ending tag. 〜ばい is all over Kyushu, with local shades of meaning.

Kyushu is not one dialect. Hakata, Kumamoto, Kagoshima and Miyazaki differ a lot, and Kagoshima speech is famously opaque to outsiders.

Local note. Fukuoka sits closest to Honshu, so Hakata-ben is the variety most travellers meet first.

Say a whole sentence

Local

ばり うまい ばい。なんしよーと？

Standard

とても おいしい よ。なにを して いるの？

It is really tasty. What are you doing?

Local

あした はやく くる ばい。

Standard

あした はやく くる よ。

I will come early tomorrow.

09

Okinawa 沖縄

Naha 沖縄県 · Okinawa

めんそーれ

The Ryukyu chain, whose languages split from mainland Japanese centuries ago. Okinawan and the other Ryukyuan languages are not just accents; local speech also mixes with standard Japanese.

Local→Standard Japanese

めんそーれmensōre
→
いらっしゃいませwelcome

Heard at airports, shops and festivals across the islands.

なんくるないさnankurunaisa
→
なんとかなるit’ll work out

Don’t worry, things will sort themselves out. A whole philosophy in one phrase.

はいさいhaisai
→
こんにちはhello

A male “hello”; はいたい is the female form. Younger speakers often use こんにちは.

Okinawan is a separate Japonic language, not simply a dialect of standard Japanese. The map simplifies a multilingual chain of islands.

Local note. The Ryukyu islands stretch over 1,000 km. Naha speech, Miyako and Yaeyama are not interchangeable.

Say a whole sentence

Local

めんそーれ、なんくるないさ。

Standard

いらっしゃい、なんとかなるさ。

Welcome — it will work out.

Local

はいさい、でーじ たのしい さー。

Standard

こんにちは、とても たのしい よ。

Hello, it is really fun.

A small experiment

One idea.
Seven words.

English uses one “very” for everything. Japan reaches for a different word in almost every region. Same meaning, different music — tap a word to find it on the map.

Hokkaido とても
Chubu とても
Kansai とても
Chugoku とても
Shikoku とても / たくさん
Kyushu とても
Okinawa とても

The standard fallback is とても. Shikoku’s こじゃんと leans toward “plenty”, and Okinawa’s でーじ can also mean “serious” or “a lot”.

A good traveller’s habit

Start standard.
Then borrow the colour.

Use standard Japanese first; it is polite, portable and understood everywhere. When a local phrase comes back to you, try it — and enjoy the smile when you get the melody wrong. That is usually the point.
